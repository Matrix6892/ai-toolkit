# Northbank seed collection: research dispatch operations
Document owner: Collection Services
Status: agreed operating contract for the spring pilot
Scope: dispatch of living seed material to research institutes

## Purpose
The collection preserves rare seed accessions while supplying research material.
An accession identifies the original collection event and botanical identity.
It is not a statement that all material has a common storage history.
One accession can occupy several independently sealed storage jars.
Recipients request a species and quantity; staff select a physical jar.
The institute may receive seed from only the selected jar in that dispatch.

## Physical identities
A jar ID is assigned when seed enters a sealed storage jar.
Jar IDs never move to a different physical jar.
The seal generation is an integer attached to a jar ID.
Initial sealing assigns generation 1.
Opening the lid and resealing increments the generation by exactly one.
Moving a sealed jar between shelves does not increment its generation.
The operator records an opening before placing the jar back in storage.
The small assay port can be sampled without opening the lid.
Port sampling does not increment the seal generation.

## Quarantine rule
Every jar generation begins with a dispatch hold.
A negative viable-fungus assay authorizes dispatch from its sampled jar generation.
A negative result for another jar does not authorize this jar.
A negative result for an older seal generation does not authorize the current one.
An assay is evidence about the sampled material, not about an accession name.
A positive assay leaves that jar generation on hold.
No general accession release certificate exists in this pilot.
The same rule applies to full-jar shipments and drawn portions.
Drawing the shipment portion takes place after the dispatch decision.
A decision and draw are performed under a jar work reservation.
An opening for repacking is excluded while that reservation is held.

## Repacking
Staff sometimes need a smaller jar to recover freezer capacity.
A replacement physical jar receives a new jar ID and generation 1.
Its source accession is retained for scientific traceability.
An opening followed by return to the same jar increments that jar's generation.
Either operation starts a new dispatch hold for the affected material.
An old jar's assay can remain visible in history for audit.
History does not by itself authorize a new jar or generation.

## Laboratory policy
The assay takes between one and four working days.
A result reports one sample and one physical jar generation.
The laboratory cannot infer the current jar state at result delivery time.
A result may arrive after repacking or after a second result.
The lab may resend the same signed result after a delivery timeout.
Negative and positive results are final for the particular sample in this pilot.
Conflicting final results for the same sample are escalated to staff.
The pilot does not define a mechanism for reversing a signed final result.

## Collection workflow
Intake seals jars, prints labels and creates their registry rows.
An assay operator scans a jar and draws through the assay port.
The laboratory receives the sample under its own sample identifier.
A collection operator can continue ordinary storage work while the lab is running.
A dispatch picker scans the chosen jar before reserving work.
The application either issues a dispatch ticket or displays a hold reason.
The picker cannot bypass a hold through the normal application.
Manual quarantine exceptions are outside this pilot and require a future policy.

## Operational examples
Accession NB-71 occupies jars J-A and J-B.
Both jars share the same collection location and harvest date.
J-A was stored dry; J-B had a damaged lid before its initial sealing.
A negative J-A assay says nothing about the viable fungus in J-B.
If J-A is opened for moisture inspection, its generation changes.
A J-A generation 1 result arriving later remains useful historical evidence.
It does not authorize dispatch from J-A generation 2.
Shelf transfers of unopened J-A do not invalidate generation 1 evidence.

## Boundaries and ownership
Collection Services owns this eligibility rule and handling procedures.
Jar Registry owns jar identity, current generation and work reservations.
Assay Gateway owns receipt and validation of laboratory results.
Dispatch owns ticket creation and recipient allocation.
No service is allowed to infer biological coverage from botanical identity alone.
Staff dashboards may group jars by accession for navigation.
Scientific taxonomy corrections do not alter physical jar identity.

## Pilot limits
This contract addresses viable-fungus clearance only.
Recipient permits and seed availability are checked by other existing workflows.
The supplied design makes no claim about cross-border permit policy.
A dispatch hold does not imply that the accession should be destroyed.
The pilot measures rejected picks and time from assay receipt to eligible pick.
An unavailable eligibility decision must leave the jar in storage.
